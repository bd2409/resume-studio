## The 5 feedback and coaching frameworks at a glance

| Best for | Framework | Origin | Why it made the list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific, fair feedback | SBI | Center for Creative Leadership | Hard to argue with, easy to remember |
| Coaching conversations | GROW | Sir John Whitmore and colleagues | People own their solutions |
| Getting the tone right | Radical Candor | Kim Scott | Care and challenge together |
| Different people, different needs | Situational leadership | Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard | One style doesn't fit all |
| Moving forward | Feedforward | Marshall Goldsmith | Focuses on the next time |

## Do you need a feedback framework?

You probably do if:

- You were promoted for being great at the work, not at leading people.
- You give the same feedback repeatedly and nothing changes.
- People leave your one-to-ones unsure what you meant.
- Your team brings you every problem to solve.
- You save everything for the annual appraisal.

## Being nice isn't the same as being kind.

The most common trap isn't harsh feedback. It's no feedback at all: staying quiet to avoid discomfort while someone keeps making the same mistake. Kim Scott calls this "ruinous empathy". Clear, caring feedback is the kinder choice.

## 1. SBI

- **Situation:** when and where.
- **Behaviour:** what the person did, observable and specific.
- **Impact:** the effect on you, the team, the customer or the result.

> "In yesterday's sprint review (situation), you presented the delay without a recovery plan (behaviour). The client left unsure about the launch date and has escalated (impact). What happened, and what should we send them today?"

**Good to know:** SBI works for praise too. "In the board meeting, you answered the CFO's question on margins with exact numbers. It built real confidence in our plan."

**Best for:** any manager who finds feedback conversations awkward.

## 2. GROW

| Step | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Goal | What do you want to achieve? What would success look like? |
| Reality | What's happening now? What have you tried? |
| Options | What could you do? What else? |
| Way forward | What will you do, by when? What support do you need? |

**Good to know:** resist giving the answer in the Options step. Ask "what else?" at least twice.

**Best for:** managers who have a queue of people outside their door.

## 3. Radical Candor

Plots feedback on two axes: **care personally** and **challenge directly**.

- **Radical Candor:** high care, high challenge. The goal.
- **Ruinous Empathy:** high care, low challenge. The most common trap.
- **Obnoxious Aggression:** low care, high challenge.
- **Manipulative Insincerity:** low care, low challenge.

**Good to know:** caring is shown over time, not by softening one message. People accept challenge from managers who have clearly backed them before.

**Best for:** checking the tone of a difficult message before you deliver it.

## 4. Situational leadership

Match your style to the person's competence and commitment **on this task**: directing, coaching, supporting or delegating. The same person can need delegating on one task and directing on another.

## 5. Feedforward

Ask for, and give, suggestions for the future rather than dissecting the past. "For the next client pitch, I'd open with their numbers, not ours."

## How to run a difficult feedback conversation

1. Prepare your SBI in writing.
2. Meet privately, soon after the event.
3. Share the SBI, then stop and ask for their view.
4. Listen without interrupting.
5. Move into GROW: what do they want to do differently?
6. Agree one next step and a date to check in.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the SBI feedback model?
SBI stands for Situation, Behaviour, Impact. You describe when and where something happened, the specific behaviour, and its effect, then invite the person's view.

### What is the GROW model in coaching?
GROW is a four-step coaching conversation: Goal, Reality, Options and Way forward. The manager asks questions so the person finds and commits to their own solution.

### How do I give negative feedback without demotivating someone?
Be specific about behaviour and impact, show you care about their success, ask for their perspective, and agree a clear next step. Avoid labels about personality.

### How often should managers give feedback?
Little and often. Short, specific feedback every week works better than a long list at the annual appraisal.

### What is Radical Candor?
Radical Candor, from Kim Scott, means caring personally about someone while challenging them directly, rather than staying silent or being harsh.

### Should feedback be given in writing?
Have the conversation in person or on a call first. A short written follow-up of the agreed next step helps both of you remember it.
