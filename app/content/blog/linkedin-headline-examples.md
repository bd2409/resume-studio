Your LinkedIn headline appears next to your name everywhere: in search results, comments, connection requests and recruiter tools. It is one of the most important lines in your professional life, and most people leave it as their current job title.

## The formula

**Target role | 2 to 3 specialisms | One proof point**

- **Target role**: the title recruiters search for, at your level.
- **Specialisms**: skills, industries or tools that set you apart.
- **Proof point**: a short result or credential, if it fits.

LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters. Use them.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Only your current title** ("Manager at XYZ Ltd") tells recruiters little.
- **"Aspiring", "fresher looking for opportunities" or "open to work"** weaken your positioning. Use LinkedIn's Open to Work setting instead.
- **Buzzwords** like "passionate", "dynamic" or "go-getter" take space without adding meaning.
- **Too many specialisms.** Two or three focused ones are stronger than ten.
- **Emojis everywhere.** One is fine in some fields; a row of them looks unprofessional.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a LinkedIn headline be?
LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters. Most strong headlines use 100 to 200 characters to fit a role, specialisms and a proof point.

### Should my LinkedIn headline be my job title?
Not only your job title. Include the role you want to be found for plus your specialisms, because recruiters search by both.

### Should I write "open to work" in my headline?
It's better to use LinkedIn's Open to Work feature, which can be shown to recruiters only, and keep your headline focused on your value.

### How often should I update my LinkedIn headline?
Whenever your target role, specialisms or key achievements change, and at least once a year.
