If you are over 35 or 40, you bring what younger candidates can't: judgement, a network, depth in your domain and a record of results. Yet many experienced professionals send resumes that hide all of that under two decades of job descriptions.

The goal is simple: make a recruiter see a strong, current professional in the first ten seconds.

## 1. Focus on the last 10 to 15 years

Recruiters care most about what you did recently. Give your last two or three roles detailed achievement bullets. For roles older than 15 years, either list them in one line each (company, title) under "Earlier career", or leave them out if they don't support your target role.

This also keeps you to **two pages**, which is the maximum recruiters expect even at senior levels.

## 2. Remove details that date you or invite bias

- **Date of birth, age, marital status, religion and photo.** They are not needed on a modern resume.
- **Graduation years** for degrees more than 15 years old. The degree and institution are enough.
- **Career objective.** Replace it with a three to four line summary of results.
- **"References available on request"** and outdated sections like "Hobbies" (unless directly relevant).
- **An old email domain** you no longer use for work. A simple Gmail or your own domain looks current.

## 3. Lead with results and leadership

Experienced candidates often list responsibilities: "Responsible for plant operations." Instead, show scale and outcomes:

> "Led operations for a 600-person plant, cutting cost per unit by 12% and lost-time injuries to zero over two years."

Include the size of teams, budgets, revenue, regions or customers you've handled. At senior levels, scope is part of the story.

## 4. Show you are current

The unspoken worry about some experienced candidates is whether they will adapt to new tools and ways of working. Answer it on paper:

- List current tools you genuinely use (ERP, CRM, analytics, collaboration tools).
- Add recent certifications or courses, especially from the last three years.
- Mention a real example of using AI or automation at work and its result, for example: "Introduced an AI-assisted weekly reporting process, cutting preparation time from 4 hours to 45 minutes."

## 5. Modernise the format

- One column, clean fonts, standard headings, no tables or text boxes, so applicant tracking systems can read it.
- A headline under your name that states your target role and two strengths.
- Your LinkedIn URL, and a LinkedIn profile that matches your resume.

## 6. Address "overqualified" before they ask

If you're applying to a role at your level or slightly below, use your summary to explain the fit: the problems you want to solve, why this role, and how your experience shortens their ramp-up time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I remove my graduation year from my resume?
For degrees more than about 15 years old, it is common and acceptable to show the degree and institution without the year. Your work history already shows your experience.

### How far back should a resume go for someone over 40?
Detail the last 10 to 15 years. Summarise earlier roles in a line each, or omit them if they don't support the job you want.

### Is a two-page resume okay for senior roles?
Yes. Two pages is the norm for experienced professionals. Going beyond two pages usually means older roles or duties need trimming.

### Should I mention my age on my resume in India?
No. Date of birth and age are not required on a modern resume, and leaving them out keeps the focus on your skills and results.
