| Handling conflict | Thomas-Kilmann modes | Kenneth Thomas and Ralph Kilmann, 1974 | Five styles, each with a right time |

## Do you need self-awareness models?

You'll find them useful if:

- Feedback about you surprises you.
- Conflicts keep repeating with the same people.
- You react quickly and regret it later.
- Your team seems stressed by changes you think are small.
- You're coaching people who are struggling to learn something new.

## You can't lead others well until you can read yourself.

Strategy and frameworks matter, but most leaders who struggle do so because of blind spots, reactions and relationships. These models give you language for things that are usually left unsaid.

## 1. Johari Window

Four quadrants: Open, Blind, Hidden and Unknown. Grow the Open area by asking for feedback and sharing appropriately.

**Good to know:** the Blind spot grows with seniority, because people tell senior leaders less.

**Best for:** new managers and new teams. Full guide: [the Johari Window explained](/blog/johari-window-explained).

## 2. Ladder of Inference

We climb from **observable data** to **selected data**, **meanings**, **assumptions**, **conclusions**, **beliefs** and **actions**, often in seconds.

> A team member is late to two meetings. You conclude they're not committed. In fact, they're covering for a sick colleague.

**Good to know:** ask "What data am I basing this on?" before acting on a conclusion about someone.

**Best for:** leaders who make quick judgements about people. Full guide: [the Ladder of Inference](/blog/ladder-of-inference).

## 3. Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes

Five styles based on assertiveness and cooperativeness: **competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating**. Each fits some situations.

**Good to know:** most people overuse one or two styles. Knowing your default lets you choose deliberately.

**Best for:** managers handling disagreements. Full guide: [Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes](/blog/thomas-kilmann-conflict-modes).

## 4. Emotional intelligence

Goleman's five components: **self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills**. The first two are about you; the last two are about others.

## 5. SCARF

David Rock's model of five social needs: **Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness**. A threat to any of them can trigger a strong reaction. A reorganisation can threaten all five at once, which is why small changes sometimes cause big resistance.

## 6. Drama Triangle

Stephen Karpman's three roles in unhealthy conflict: **Persecutor, Victim, Rescuer**. People switch roles but stay stuck. David Emerald's "Empowerment Dynamic" offers healthier alternatives: Challenger, Creator and Coach.

## 7. Transactional Analysis

Eric Berne's idea that we speak from three ego states: **Parent** (critical or nurturing), **Adult** (rational) and **Child** (free or adapted). A manager speaking as a critical Parent often gets a defensive Child in return. Speaking Adult-to-Adult works best at work.

## 8. Conscious competence

Learners move from **unconsciously incompetent** (don't know what they don't know) to **consciously incompetent**, **consciously competent** and finally **unconsciously competent**. The second stage feels uncomfortable, which is when people most need encouragement.

## 9. Growth mindset

Carol Dweck's research distinguishes believing abilities are fixed from believing they can be developed. Praise effort, strategy and progress, not just talent.

## 10. Circle of Influence

Stephen Covey separates your **Circle of Concern** (everything you care about) from your **Circle of Influence** (what you can affect). Effective people focus on the second, and it grows.

## How to build your self-awareness in 30 days

1. **Week 1:** ask three colleagues, "What's one thing I do that helps you, and one thing I could do differently?"
2. **Week 2:** notice one snap judgement a day, and walk it back down the Ladder of Inference.
3. **Week 3:** identify your default conflict style, and try a different one once.
4. **Week 4:** list your current worries, cross out what's outside your influence, and act on one item that's inside it.
5. Write down what you learned and share it with your manager or mentor.

## Frequently asked questions

### What models are similar to the Johari Window?
Related self-awareness models include the Ladder of Inference, Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes, Goleman's emotional intelligence, the SCARF model, Transactional Analysis and the Drama Triangle.

### Why is self-awareness important for leaders?
Self-aware leaders understand how they affect others, manage their reactions, and adapt their style, which builds trust and better decisions.

### Which model helps with workplace conflict?
The Thomas-Kilmann model helps you choose a conflict style deliberately, and the Drama Triangle helps you spot unhealthy blame patterns.

### What is the SCARF model?
SCARF, from David Rock, describes five social needs (Status, Certainty, Autonomy, Relatedness, Fairness) that, when threatened, trigger strong reactions at work.

### Are personality tests like MBTI useful for leaders?
They can start useful conversations, but many psychologists question their reliability, and they shouldn't be used for hiring or promotion decisions.

### How can I improve self-awareness quickly?
Ask for specific feedback regularly, reflect on your reactions, and notice the assumptions behind your conclusions about people.
