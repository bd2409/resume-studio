By Bhargab Das, marketing strategist for startups.

Many startups treat marketing as a set of activities: some posts, a few ads, a website refresh. Growth comes from an **engine**: a system where each part feeds the next, and you can see what's working each week. Here's the order I use when building one.

## 1. Positioning: who it's for and why you win

Answer three questions in one line each:

- **Who** is your best customer? (Be specific: "D2C founders doing Rs 1 to 10 crore a year", not "businesses".)
- **What problem** do you solve that they care about now?
- **Why you**, rather than the alternatives, including doing nothing?

If your team can't say this the same way, no amount of ad spend will fix it.

## 2. Brand identity and story

Your brand is the promise people remember. Define:

- A short brand story: the problem, why you started, what you believe.
- Tone of voice, a simple visual identity, and consistent naming.
- For physical products, packaging that sells on the shelf and in a thumbnail.

## 3. A website that converts

Your website should do three jobs in the first screen: **explain** what you do, **prove** it works (numbers, logos, reviews), and **convert** with one clear action. Then add pages for each key use case, answers to common questions, and fast load times on mobile.

## 4. One or two acquisition channels

Pick channels that match where your customers already are:

- **Search** (SEO and Google Ads) when people already look for your solution.
- **Social and performance marketing** (Meta, YouTube, LinkedIn) when you need to create demand.
- **Marketplaces and quick commerce** for consumer products.
- **Partnerships and outbound** for B2B.

Go deep on one or two before adding more. Measure cost per lead or sale weekly.

## 5. Content that compounds

Publish useful content consistently: guides, case studies, founder posts, videos. Answer the questions your customers ask. Good content helps search engines and AI assistants recommend you, and makes every other channel cheaper.

## 6. Measurement you actually use

Track a short weekly dashboard:

- Traffic and leads by channel
- Conversion rate on key pages
- Customer acquisition cost and payback
- Repeat purchase or retention

Review it every week, decide one or two changes, and repeat.

## Common mistakes

- Spending on ads before positioning and the website are ready.
- Running five channels at once with no clear owner.
- Measuring vanity metrics (followers, impressions) instead of leads and revenue.
- Changing the brand message every month.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should a startup start marketing?
Before launch, with positioning and a simple website. Scale paid marketing once you have a clear message, a page that converts and early signs of product-market fit.

### How much should a startup spend on marketing?
It depends on stage, margins and goals. Start with a small, measured budget on one or two channels, and increase spend where you can see a healthy cost per customer.

### Should a startup hire an agency or build an in-house team?
Early on, a senior strategist plus specialist freelancers or an agency can work well. Build in-house once you know which channels work and need daily ownership.

### What's the difference between brand marketing and performance marketing?
Brand marketing builds long-term recognition and trust; performance marketing drives measurable actions now. Startups need both, in the right balance for their stage.
