## The 7 strategy frameworks at a glance

| Best for | Framework | Origin | Why it made the list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Picking your customer | STP | Classic marketing practice | Focus beats broad messaging |
| Understanding why people buy | Jobs to Be Done | Clayton Christensen | Reveals the real competition |
| Judging a market | Porter's Five Forces | Michael Porter | Shows where profits leak |
| Choosing growth options | Ansoff matrix | Igor Ansoff | Ranks options by risk |
| Shaping the offer | 4Ps | E. Jerome McCarthy | Keeps product, price and channel consistent |
| Compounding growth | Flywheel | Jim Collins | Each step feeds the next |
| Quick position check | SWOT | Business strategy practice | Fast summary before planning |

## Do you need strategy frameworks?

They help most when:

- Your messaging tries to appeal to everyone.
- You're considering a new market, product or channel.
- Marketing spend is rising but growth isn't.
- Your team disagrees about priorities.

## A framework is only useful if it changes a decision.

A beautifully filled-in SWOT that leads to no action is a slide, not a strategy. Before using any framework, write down the decision it should inform: which customer, which market, which channel, which price.

## 1. STP: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning

Divide the market, choose the segment you can win, and position yourself clearly for them.

> Segment: D2C skincare brands doing Rs 1 to 10 crore a year.
> Target: founders who run marketing themselves.
> Positioning: "The marketing team you can't hire yet."

**Good to know:** a narrow target feels risky but makes every other choice easier, from content to pricing.

**Best for:** early-stage startups with broad, unclear messaging.

## 2. Jobs to Be Done

Customers "hire" a product to make progress in their life. Ask what progress they want, not which features.

- A working professional buying a resume service is hiring it to get interviews for a better job, quickly and without embarrassment.
- The real competition may be "doing nothing" or "asking a friend".

**Good to know:** the best insights come from interviewing recent buyers about the moment they decided to buy.

**Best for:** positioning, product decisions and ad messaging.

## 3. Porter's Five Forces

Looks at rivalry, threat of new entrants, buyer power, supplier power and threat of substitutes.

**Good to know:** a market where buyers can switch easily and substitutes are everywhere needs a very sharp edge to be profitable.

**Best for:** deciding whether to enter a new market or category.

## 4. Ansoff matrix

| | Existing products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing markets** | Market penetration (lowest risk) | Product development |
| **New markets** | Market development | Diversification (highest risk) |

Most startups should win penetration first.

## 5. The 4Ps

**Product, Price, Place, Promotion.** A premium product sold at discount prices through the wrong channel will confuse customers. Check all four point the same way.

## 6. The flywheel

A loop where each step pushes the next: better product, happier customers, more reviews and referrals, lower acquisition cost, more to invest in the product. Find the slowest step and invest there.

## 7. SWOT

Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. Useful as a six-monthly check, but only if each point leads to an action.

## How to build your strategy in a weekend

1. Write your STP in three lines.
2. Interview five recent customers using Jobs to Be Done questions.
3. Run Five Forces on your current market.
4. Place each growth idea on the Ansoff matrix.
5. Check your 4Ps are consistent.
6. Draw your flywheel and pick the one step to improve this quarter.

## Our verdict: where I'd start

| What to look for | Why it matters | STP + Jobs to Be Done + flywheel |
|---|---|---|
| Starts with the customer | Everything else depends on it | ✓ |
| Leads to clear choices | Focus for a small team | ✓ |
| Shows where to invest | Spend goes where it compounds | ✓ |

For most early-stage founders, STP and Jobs to Be Done fix the message, and the flywheel shows where to invest. Use Five Forces and Ansoff when you're weighing a new market.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the most important strategy frameworks for a startup?
STP and Jobs to Be Done to pick and understand your customer, the Ansoff matrix to plan growth and the flywheel to see how growth compounds. SWOT and Five Forces help with periodic reviews and new markets.

### Is SWOT analysis still useful?
Yes, as a quick summary, but only if it leads to specific actions.

### What is the Jobs to Be Done framework?
It's the idea that customers "hire" products to make progress in a specific situation. Understanding that job helps you design, position and price your product.

### What's the difference between strategy and marketing frameworks?
Strategy frameworks such as Five Forces and Ansoff help decide where to compete. Marketing frameworks such as STP and the 4Ps help decide how to win customers there.

### What is a business flywheel?
A flywheel is a loop where each part of the business strengthens the next, so growth builds momentum over time. The idea was popularised by Jim Collins.

### When should a founder get outside help with strategy?
When growth stalls, when you're about to raise or spend significantly on marketing, or when the team can't agree on the target customer.
