Sending the same resume to every job is the most common reason good candidates don't hear back. Recruiters spend seconds on a first scan, and an applicant tracking system searches for specific skills. A tailored resume tells both, immediately, that you fit this role.

Tailoring does **not** mean inventing experience. It means choosing which of your real achievements to highlight, and describing them in the employer's language.

## Step 1: Pull out the must-haves (5 minutes)

Read the job description twice and write down:

- **The target title** and level (for example, "Senior Product Manager").
- **Must-have skills and tools**, usually in "Requirements" or repeated across the posting.
- **Outcomes they care about**: growth, cost reduction, compliance, launches, team building.
- **Nice-to-haves**: useful, but not decisive.

Most postings have 8 to 15 real must-haves. Those are your checklist.

## Step 2: Rewrite the top third (10 minutes)

Recruiters read the top of page one most carefully, so start there.

- **Headline**: match the role. "Senior Product Manager | Payments & Fintech | B2B SaaS" beats "Product professional".
- **Summary**: three to four lines with your years of experience, the domain, and two or three results that relate to *this* job's outcomes.
- **Key competencies**: reorder so the job's must-haves come first, using their wording where it's accurate.

## Step 3: Reorder and reword your bullets (10 minutes)

For your latest one or two roles:

1. Move the bullets most relevant to this job to the top.
2. Reword them with the employer's terms. If they say "vendor management" and you wrote "handled suppliers", use their phrase.
3. Make sure each bullet shows a result: a number, a scale or a before-and-after.

Older roles need fewer changes. Trim bullets that don't help this application.

## Step 4: Check coverage (5 minutes)

Go back to your must-have list. For each one, find where your resume shows it. If something is missing:

- If you have the experience, add it to a bullet or your competencies.
- If you don't, leave it out. Mention how you'd close the gap in your cover letter or interview instead.

## A simple before-and-after

**Before:** "Responsible for managing a team and handling client accounts."

**After (for a key account manager role):** "Managed 14 enterprise accounts worth Rs 22 crore a year, growing renewals from 81% to 93% while leading a team of 5."

Same job, same facts, far more relevant.

## Keep a master resume

Keep one long "master" document with every role, achievement and number you can think of. For each application, copy it and cut it down to the best two pages for that job. Tailoring then takes 30 minutes instead of a weekend.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it okay to change my resume for every job?
Yes, and you should. Changing emphasis, order and wording for each role is normal and expected. Changing facts such as titles, dates or numbers is not.

### How many keywords should I include?
Cover the job's must-have skills that are genuinely true for you, in context. Stuffing keywords or repeating them unnaturally makes the resume worse for human readers.

### Should I tailor my LinkedIn profile too?
Your LinkedIn profile can't be tailored for every job, so write it for the kind of role you want next, with the skills recruiters in that field search for.
