## The five conflict styles at a glance

| Style | Assertive? | Cooperative? | Use it when | Overuse leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competing | High | Low | Emergencies, unpopular but necessary decisions | Resentment, people stop speaking up |
| Collaborating | High | High | Important issues, both sides' needs matter, time allows | Slow decisions on small matters |
| Compromising | Medium | Medium | Equal power, a quick workable deal is needed | Half-solutions nobody loves |
| Avoiding | Low | Low | Trivial issues, emotions need to cool | Problems grow unseen |
| Accommodating | Low | High | You're wrong, or the relationship matters more | Being taken for granted |

## Do you need this model?

It's useful if:

- The same disagreements keep coming back.
- You either avoid conflict or win every argument, and neither feels right.
- Your team is polite in meetings and frustrated afterwards.
- You're mediating between two people or departments.

## Avoiding conflict isn't the same as keeping the peace.

Many teams, especially in hierarchical workplaces, treat disagreement as disrespect. The result is quiet resistance: people nod in the meeting and do something else afterwards. Healthy teams disagree openly about ideas, then commit.

## 1. Competing

Pushing for your position, using authority or argument.

- Right for a safety issue, a crisis, or a decision only you can make.
- Wrong as a default; people stop sharing bad news.

**Good to know:** if you compete, explain why, and acknowledge the other view.

**Best for:** urgent, high-stakes decisions.

## 2. Collaborating

Working together to find a solution that fully meets both sides' needs.

- Takes time and trust.
- Produces the best answers on complex problems.

> Sales wants faster discounts; finance wants margin control. Collaborating might produce a pre-approved discount band, with exceptions reviewed weekly.

**Good to know:** collaborating starts with asking what each side really needs, not what they're demanding.

**Best for:** important, complex issues where both sides matter.

## 3. Compromising

Each side gives something up to reach a quick, acceptable deal.

**Good to know:** compromise is efficient, but check that you're not splitting the difference on something that deserves a proper solution.

**Best for:** time pressure and equal bargaining power.

## 4. Avoiding

Postponing or sidestepping the issue.

**Good to know:** avoiding is a valid choice when emotions are high. Say when you'll come back to it, and do.

## 5. Accommodating

Putting the other person's needs first.

**Good to know:** accommodating when you're wrong builds credibility. Accommodating every time teaches people that pushing works.

## How to handle a team conflict in five steps

1. **Name it:** "It seems we see this differently. Let's talk it through."
2. **Separate facts from interpretations** using the [Ladder of Inference](/blog/ladder-of-inference).
3. **Ask each side what they need**, not just what they want.
4. **Choose a style** based on stakes, time and relationship.
5. **Agree the decision and who owns it**, then follow up.

## Our verdict: which style should you use?

| Situation | Best style |
|---|---|
| Crisis or safety issue | Competing |
| Strategic, complex, important | Collaborating |
| Deadline, equal power | Compromising |
| Trivial or emotionally heated | Avoiding (for now) |
| You're wrong, or the relationship matters most | Accommodating |

The best leaders aren't defined by one style. They know their default, and switch deliberately.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the five Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes?
Competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding and accommodating.

### Which conflict style is the best?
None is always best. Collaborating suits important, complex issues; competing suits emergencies; avoiding suits trivial issues. Choose based on the situation.

### What is the TKI?
The Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument is a paid questionnaire that measures how often you use each of the five styles.

### What's the difference between collaborating and compromising?
Collaborating aims for a solution that fully satisfies both sides; compromising settles for a middle ground where both sides give something up.

### How do I find my default conflict style?
Think about your last three disagreements and how you handled them, or ask a trusted colleague. The formal TKI assessment is another option.

### How do managers resolve conflict between two team members?
Meet each person first, then together. Focus on facts and needs, help them find options, agree a decision and owner, and follow up.
